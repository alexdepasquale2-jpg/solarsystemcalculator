# STILL TANK — working title

A game about a room that does not wait for you.

## The setup

There is a glass case on a table. Inside it: dirt, water, heat, small bodies, paths they
wear into the dirt, and talk that moves between them like weather.

You did not build the case. You inherited it. When you boot the game, the case is already
mid-sentence.

## What is on screen

A single volume. You can walk the camera in until you see grit and drips. You can pull back
until the grit is a hillside and the drips are a river and the hillside has a cluster of
shelters.

You never load a second map. Zoom is the map.

Tools sit in a thin strip. Not a loadout. Not a class.

- add / take away matter
- raise or lower heat
- block a path
- open a path
- mark one patch with a rule ("nothing grows here," "sound carries here," "this edge is a border")
- leave a mark the bodies can copy (a shape, a noise, a habit)

That is the inventory.

## A session

You sit down. Something in the case has already gone wrong in a small way. The river undercut
a bank. A group learned to sleep on the warm side and now the cold side is empty. Two clusters
are repeating two different versions of last week.

You pick one tool. You use it once, maybe twice. Then you stop touching.

The rest of the hour is watching whether the case accepts the change or eats it.

If you quit, the case keeps running at a slow tick. Next time you open it, the bank has moved.
Someone copied your mark and got it wrong. That wrong copy is now a custom.

## What "progress" looks like

Not levels. Layers of scar.

A wall you poured becomes a ridge. A ridge becomes a road. A road becomes a reason two groups
will not cross. A reason becomes a story they tell. The story is not voiced by an actor. It is
a pattern you can point at with the camera and say: that started as a pour.

You can ruin it. The game will let you. Ruin is just another scar.

## What it feels like to play well

You spend more time still than busy.

The skill is noticing which drip will become a river if you ignore it, and which drip is only a
drip. The game is honest about that. It does not circle the important drip in gold.

When you do act, the case should feel slightly heavier afterward. One more fact the room has to
live with.

## What it is not

Not a village to babysit.
Not a puzzle with a correct door.
Not a novel that unlocks if you grind.
Not a toy that resets when you blink.

It is a room with weather in it. You are the person who comes back and decides whether to touch
the weather.

## The line on the box

A case of living weather. You change one thing. You live with the next version.

# Emergence, stated plainly

Emergence is not magic and it is not "the game got smart."

It is this fact: you specify only local rules; the thing that shows up later is a pattern no
rule named.

A grain falls if the cell under it is empty. Nobody wrote "dune." Dunes appear anyway. That
gap — between the sentence you typed and the shape you did not type — is the whole subject.

## The only ingredients

Emergence needs four cheap parts. Miss one and you get a screensaver or a spreadsheet.

1. **Many similar pieces.** Too few and you can still hold every interaction in your head. Then
   nothing "emerges"; you just calculated.
2. **Local rules only.** A piece may see neighbors, heat, a number, a mark. It may not see the
   plan. If a unit has a flag `formCircleNow`, that circle is authored, not emergent.
3. **Coupling.** Outputs become inputs. Heat moves matter. Matter blocks heat. Paths wear. Worn
   paths attract more traffic. Without feedback you get weather that never learns.
4. **A middle temperature.** All random: noise, no memory. All locked: crystal, no news.
   Emergence lives in the band where a pattern can form and also break.

That band is the design problem. Everything else is decoration.

## Three grades, so the word stops meaning "cool"

- **Aggregation.** Piles, flocks, traffic jams. The pieces do not change. Only their arrangement
  does. Sand dunes. Birds. Still Tank's first ten minutes.
- **Differentiation.** The pieces take on roles because of where they sat. A cell at the edge
  becomes wall; a cell in the middle becomes gut. Same genome, different job. This is when the
  tank starts looking alive.
- **Record.** The pattern writes into the medium and the medium outlives the pieces. A trail. A
  ridge. A rumor that survives the mouths that made it. This is the only grade that gives you
  history instead of a loop.

Most "emergent" games stop at aggregation and call it a day. The interesting game lives in record.

## How a pattern actually starts

You do not spawn it. You make a bias and wait.

A slight slope. A slightly warmer patch. A mark one body copies a little wrong. Amplification
does the rest: more traffic deepens the path, the path pulls more traffic. Or damping kills it:
heat spreads, the advantage dies, the almost-pattern is forgotten.

Your job as designer of the case is not to invent the dune. It is to decide which tiny biases
are allowed to amplify, and which the physics will flatten.

That is why one tool used once is the right verb. A tool is a bias. The session is whether the
bias caught.

## Scales have to leak

If grit cannot become a hill, and a hill cannot become a border, you do not have a world. You
have stacked minigames.

Leakage is the mechanism:

- many grains → a bank
- a bank → a path of least resistance
- a path → two groups that stop crossing
- the stopped crossing → two stories about why

No storyteller. The story is what leakage looks like when you zoom out and name it.

The hard part is keeping the leak lossy. Perfect translation up the scale is just a chart. Dirty
translation — wrong copies, heat scars, missing witnesses — is how you get a world that remembers
badly, which is the only kind of memory that feels like life.

## Failure modes (these are the real enemies)

- **Script wearing a mustache.** A hidden director fires "event: flood" when a meter hits 7. That
  is not emergence. That is a cutscene with extra steps.
- **Explosion of degrees of freedom.** Every piece unique, every rule special. You cannot read
  the result, so you cannot tell a pattern from a mess. Players call it depth. It is fog.
- **Frozen success.** You find one stable pretty state and the tank sits there forever. No news.
  You have built a paperweight.
- **Noise success.** You add so much jitter that nothing can hold a shape. Also no news. You have
  built television snow.
- **Player as author of everything.** If the only patterns that appear are the ones the player
  drew on purpose, the case is a paint program.

The usable region is narrow: few rule types, many instances, leaky scales, enough noise to break
crystals, not enough to erase scars.

## What "play" is, once you take this seriously

Play is not commanding units.

Play is:

1. notice a bias that is about to amplify
2. decide whether to feed it, cut it, or leave it
3. read the scar it leaves at the next scale

Skill is prediction across a scale jump. "If I warm this corner, the bodies will pack, the packed
bodies will wear a road, the road will become a border in three days." You can be wrong. Being
wrong is data.

A good session can be twenty minutes of not touching, because not touching is how you learn which
patterns are self-holding.

## The honest limit

You will not get a mind for free. Local rules can give you dunes, organs, roads, customs, even
languages of habit. They do not automatically give you someone home inside the pattern. If you
want that, you are asking for a second thing: an inner loop that models the outer loop. That is
possible. It is not implied.

Do not advertise a soul because a flock turned.

What you can get, without lying: a case that writes its own weather, then writes a bad memory of
that weather into the dirt, then forces you to live with the memory. That is already more world
than most games bother to run.

Emergence is just that stubbornness — the pattern that keeps existing after the hands that started
it have left the room.
